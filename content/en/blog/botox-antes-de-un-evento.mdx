December brings parties, dinners, graduations and photos, and many patients ask me the same thing: **when should I get Botox before an event** so it looks natural and doesn't catch me off guard? The answer comes down to one thing: **Botox doesn't work overnight**. This guide sums up what I explain in my office in Bogotá so you arrive at your celebrations with your result already in place.

## What is Botox and why isn't it instant?

Botox (botulinum toxin) is a protein that **temporarily relaxes the muscles** that create expression lines: forehead, frown lines and crow's feet. It doesn't fill or change your features: it softens the lines that appear when you frown or smile.

The muscle takes time to "get the message." According to the product's official U.S. prescribing information, the effect **starts 1 to 2 days** after treatment and **builds during the first week** (1). The full result shows at around **2 weeks**. To learn more about the procedure, read my guide [Botox: when to start and what to expect](/en/blog/botox-cuando-empezar-que-esperar/).

## How many days before an event should you get Botox?

My recommendation is to get it **3 to 4 weeks before** your event. That way:

- **Days 1-2:** the effect begins.
- **Days 7-14:** the result settles and looks natural.
- **Day 14:** follow-up visit. If one brow sits a little higher or an area needs more softening, I do a **touch-up**.
- **Weeks 3-4:** you arrive at your event with a stable result and no injection marks.

**What if there are only 2 weeks left?** It can still be done, but there's no room left for a touch-up. **Less than a week?** I don't recommend it: the effect may not be complete, and if a bruise appears, it won't have time to fade.

## How long does it last?

The effect lasts **approximately 4 months** (1). If you get it in late November, it carries you through the holidays, the January vacation and much of the start of the year.

## How to get a natural result

- **Less is more.** I prefer measured doses that soften lines without taking away your expression. You can always add more at the touch-up; you can't take it away.
- **Treat what truly bothers you.** Not every area needs Botox.
- **Pair it with glowing skin.** If you want "glow" for photos, Botox won't give you that. A gentle [chemical peel](/en/services/peeling-quimico/) or hydration with [hyaluronic acid](/en/services/acido-hialuronico/) works better, also planned weeks ahead.

## Care before and after Botox

**Before:**
- If your doctor allows it, avoid aspirin, ibuprofen and supplements such as omega-3 or vitamin E for a few days beforehand: **they increase the risk of bruising**. Never stop a prescribed medication without asking your doctor.
- Arrive with clean skin, without makeup.

**After:**
- Don't massage or rub the area the same day.
- Avoid intense exercise, saunas and alcohol for the first 24 hours.
- If a bruise appears, you can cover it with makeup the next day, and it fades in a few days.

## Is it safe? Side effects and who should NOT get Botox

It's one of the most studied cosmetic procedures. A review of 32 clinical trials with **more than 9,600 patients** concluded that its safety profile is acceptable (2). Another analysis found that most side effects on the forehead and frown lines are **mild and temporary**, such as headache or redness at the injection site (3).

The most feared effect is a **drooping eyelid**: in the product's trials it occurred in about **3%** of people treated for frown lines, and it's temporary (1). Good technique greatly reduces that risk.

**You should not get Botox if:**
- You are **pregnant or breastfeeding**.
- You have a **neuromuscular disease**, such as myasthenia gravis.
- You have an **infection** or inflammation in the area.
- You are taking certain antibiotics (aminoglycosides): mention it at your visit.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many days before an event should I get Botox?** Ideally 3 to 4 weeks before. That way the effect is complete and there's time for a touch-up.

**How long does Botox take to work?** It starts to show 1 to 2 days later, and the full result appears at around 2 weeks.

**Can I get Botox a week before my event?** It's risky: the effect may not be complete and there's no time to correct it. Better to allow at least 2 weeks.

**Can I drink alcohol after Botox?** Avoid it for the first 24 hours, because it increases the risk of bruising. After that, you can toast with peace of mind.

**Will people notice I had Botox?** If it's done well, no. The goal is for you to look rested, not "frozen."
