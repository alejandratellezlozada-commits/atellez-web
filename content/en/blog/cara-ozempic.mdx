You lost weight with Ozempic, Mounjaro, the so-called "peptides" or another medication, and you feel better… but when you look in the mirror, your **face looks thinner, tired or saggy**. Social media calls this **Ozempic face**, and it's one of the most common reasons patients see me in Bogotá lately. The good news: there's an explanation, and it can be treated. This guide sums up what I explain in my office.

## What is Ozempic face?

**Ozempic face** is not a disease. It's the popular name for the changes that show up in the face when you lose a lot of weight in a short time:

- **Hollow cheeks** and flatter cheekbones
- **Deeper under-eye circles** and hollow temples
- **Deeper lines** from the nose to the mouth (nasolabial folds)
- **Looser skin** on the jawline, under the chin and on the neck
- A **more aged or tired-looking face**

Ozempic and Wegovy (semaglutide) and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) are medications for diabetes and obesity. In large trials, people lost an average of **15% of their body weight** with semaglutide (1) and **up to 21%** with tirzepatide (2). That's a huge loss, and the face shows it too.

## Why does the face sag after weight loss?

The face has **fat pads** that give it volume and support the skin. When you lose weight, those pads shrink. If the skin can't adjust in time, it's left **with extra slack and sags**.

A review published in 2026 in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* found that the midface loses roughly **7% of its volume for every 10 kg** of weight lost. The loss is greater in the superficial fat, right under the skin (3).

An important point: **it's not unique to Ozempic**. The same changes happen after bariatric surgery or a very strict diet. What matters most is **how much weight you lose**, your **age** and the **quality of your skin** (3, 4). After 40, the skin has less collagen and has a harder time bouncing back.

## How to prevent Ozempic face

If you're starting treatment or you're partway through, these steps help protect your skin:

- **Don't stop your medication on your own.** The health benefits far outweigh the cosmetic change. Any adjustment should be decided with your prescribing doctor.
- **Eat enough protein** and do **strength training**. They help you keep muscle, not just lose fat.
- **Sunscreen every day.** The sun breaks down collagen and speeds up sagging.
- **Retinoids at night** to stimulate collagen. I explain how to start in my [retinoids guide](/en/blog/retinoides-guia-completa/).
- **See a dermatologist early.** It's easier to manage the change along the way than to correct it at the end.

## Treatments for facial sagging after weight loss

There is no single treatment. An international expert consensus published in 2026 recommends **assessing each area of the face separately** and tailoring the plan to age, sex and how much weight was lost (5). These are the options, from gentlest to most intensive.

### Collagen biostimulators

These are injections that **prompt your skin to make its own collagen**, such as poly-L-lactic acid (Sculptra). Results appear gradually, over 2 to 3 months, and look natural. A systematic review found that the effect of poly-L-lactic acid lasts **up to 25 months** (6). Usually **2 or 3 sessions** are needed. Read more in my article on [Sculptra and facial biostimulation](/en/blog/sculptra-bioestimulacion-facial/).

### Hyaluronic acid

It restores volume **right away** in the areas that hollow out the most: cheekbones, temples, under-eyes and jawline. Done well, it **doesn't "puff up" the face**: it supports it. The effect lasts on average 6 to 18 months, depending on the area and the product. See how I work with [hyaluronic acid](/en/services/acido-hialuronico/).

### Skin quality treatments

When there are also **fine lines, dark spots or uneven texture**, a [CO2 laser](/en/services/laser-co2/) or a [chemical peel](/en/services/peeling-quimico/) improves the skin's surface and stimulates collagen.

### Combining treatments

In practice, the best results usually come from **combining**: a biostimulator for firmness, hyaluronic acid for volume and a surface treatment for texture. Evidence in patients who lost weight with these medications is still limited and comes mostly from studies without a comparison group (3). That's why every plan is tailored.

## When is the best time to treat it?

My usual advice is to **wait until your weight is stable**, or close to your goal. If we treat while you're still losing weight, your face will keep changing and you may need touch-ups sooner. In the meantime, you can start with skin care and, in some cases, with biostimulators.

## When should you see a dermatologist?

Book a visit if your face has changed more than you expected, if you look **tired or older** even though you feel well, or if you're considering fillers and don't know where to start. In my office I assess your skin, your fat pads and where you are in your treatment, and I propose a step-by-step plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Ozempic face permanent?** Not entirely. If you regain weight, some volume comes back. If you keep the weight off, the sagging can be improved with dermatologic treatments.

**How can I avoid Ozempic face?** By eating enough protein, doing strength training, wearing sunscreen and seeing a dermatologist from the start of treatment.

**Does Mounjaro also cause Ozempic face?** Yes. Any large, fast weight loss can cause it, whether from tirzepatide, semaglutide, surgery or diet.

**When can I get fillers if I'm still losing weight?** Ideally once your weight is stable or close to your goal, so the result lasts longer.

**Should I stop my medication to avoid losing facial volume?** No. Never stop it without talking to your prescribing doctor: your face can be treated, and your health comes first.
