Acne is the most common skin disease in the world. Although it mainly affects teenagers, it can appear at any age and has a significant impact on quality of life and self-esteem. Understanding **what type of acne you have** is the first step to choosing the right treatment.

## What is acne?

Acne is inflammation of the pilosebaceous follicle (the canal where hair grows) caused by a combination of three main factors:

1. **Excess production of sebum** (oil) by the sebaceous glands
2. **Clogging of the follicle** by dead cells (follicular hyperkeratosis)
3. **Overgrowth of the bacterium** *Cutibacterium acnes* (formerly called *Propionibacterium acnes*)

Hormonal, genetic and dietary factors, as well as stress, can modulate these three components and make acne worse.

## Types of acne by lesion

### Non-inflammatory (comedonal) acne

This is the mildest type. It is characterized by:

- **Blackheads (open comedones):** the follicle is blocked and the sebum oxidizes on contact with air, turning black
- **Whiteheads (closed comedones):** the follicle is completely blocked with no contact with air

This type of acne responds well to topical retinoids (adapalene, tretinoin) and salicylic acid.

### Mild to moderate inflammatory acne

This includes:

- **Papules:** red, raised lesions without pus
- **Pustules:** lesions with visible pus inside (typical "pimples")

It requires topical treatment with benzoyl peroxide, topical antibiotics (clindamycin) and/or retinoids. In moderate cases an oral antibiotic may be needed.

### Severe (nodulocystic) acne

This is the most serious type:

- **Nodules:** deep, painful lesions without surface pus
- **Cysts:** encapsulated, pus-filled lesions that can leave significant scars

This type **always requires dermatological care**. The treatment of choice is isotretinoin (Accutane), under strict medical supervision.

### Acne conglobata

A very severe form with multiple interconnected cysts, nodules and tracts under the skin. It requires specialized treatment and close follow-up.

## Acne by age

### Neonatal and infant acne

It affects 20% of newborns. It is benign and resolves on its own within weeks.

### Teenage acne

The most common. The hormonal changes of puberty are the main trigger. It can be mild, moderate or severe.

### Adult acne

It mainly affects women over 25. It often has a hormonal component related to the menstrual cycle, contraceptives or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). It tends to appear around the jawline and chin.

## When should you see a dermatologist?

You should see a dermatologist if:

- Your acne is moderate or severe
- You've tried over-the-counter products with no improvement in 8–12 weeks
- Your acne is leaving scars
- Acne affects your self-esteem or quality of life
- You have acne in atypical areas (back, chest, buttocks)
- Your acne is mainly on the jawline (possible hormonal component)

## What treatments are available?

The treatment plan depends on the type and severity of the acne. A consultation with Dr. Téllez may include:

**Topical treatments:**
- Retinoids (adapalene, tretinoin)
- Benzoyl peroxide
- Salicylic acid
- Topical antibiotics (clindamycin)
- Azelaic acid

**Systemic treatments:**
- Oral antibiotics (tetracyclines, erythromycin)
- Oral contraceptives (in women with hormonal acne)
- Spironolactone (anti-androgen)
- Isotretinoin (for severe acne)

**In-office procedures:**
- Chemical peels (glycolic, salicylic, mandelic acid)
- Professional comedone extraction
- Phototherapy
- CO2 laser for scars

## Common acne myths

**"Acne comes from not washing your face properly"** → FALSE. Acne is not caused by poor hygiene, although over-washing can irritate the skin and make it worse.

**"Chocolate causes acne"** → The evidence is mixed. Some studies suggest that high-glycemic diets and dairy can worsen acne, but not chocolate on its own.

**"The sun improves acne"** → FALSE. A tan temporarily hides lesions, but sun damage can worsen post-acne marks and acne itself in the long run.

**"Acne goes away on its own with time"** → Untreated moderate-to-severe acne leaves permanent scars. Early treatment prevents irreversible damage.
